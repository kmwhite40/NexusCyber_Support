Bottom Line

Two platforms advanced this cycle. Carbon (federal compliance) gained a FedRAMP-style CMMC SSP generator that auto-populates from real tenant data, a consistent command-center UI, and self-accruing continuous-monitoring trends. Anchor (our in-house service desk / ITSM platform) went live in the government cloud at anchor.azurewebsites.us and now replaces the day-to-day functions we had been paying Atlassian Jira for — ticketing, SLAs, notifications, customer satisfaction, and the help-desk knowledge base.

Headline for leadership: Standing up Anchor in our own gov-cloud boundary removes per-seat Atlassian licensing for service desk and knowledge base, with an estimated ~$23K–$92K/year saved depending on agent count (≈98% lower cost — Anchor runs on ~$30/month of hosting), while keeping all data inside the FedRAMP boundary. One gap remains: we still need a project/work-management app with kanban boards to fully retire Jira.

What Shipped — High Level

Carbon — Federal Compliance

  • CMMC SSP, FedRAMP Appendix A style — covers all 110 NIST 800-171 requirements and auto-fills from the customer's own tenant data and evidence, with confidence labels so an estimate is never mistaken for a fact.
  • Consistent command-center UI across ~25 screens, and continuous-monitoring trends that accrue automatically (no manual sync) with an honest multi-cloud posture view.
  • Managed-service (CONMONaaS) documentation suite for offering Continuous Monitoring as a service.

Anchor — Service Desk / ITSM NOW LIVE

  • Live in the government cloud at anchor.azurewebsites.us — ticketing, customer portal, and agent workspace, all inside our FedRAMP boundary.
  • Email notifications fixed end-to-end — requesters and the support team are now notified on new tickets, assignments, updates, resolution, and closure (via M365 in GCC High).
  • SLA tracking — priority-based response/resolution targets on a business-hours clock; corrected a defect that falsely flagged new tickets as "breached."
  • Email-to-ticket threading — customer replies attach to the existing ticket instead of creating duplicates.
  • "Rate my experience" (CSAT) now works on resolved tickets.
  • Enterprise dashboards — Executive, SLA, Operations, Team, Customer Portfolio, Posture & Compliance, On-call & Change.
  • Branding + documentation — official Anchor identity applied site-wide, plus three new guides: Administrator, End-User, and Intune device-enrollment (Company Portal + Outlook Classic for GovCloud).

Estimated net savings: ~$22,700/year at 25 agents (≈98%). Anchor's only run cost is ~$30/month of hosting, and it adds no per-seat cost, so savings scale almost entirely with headcount: roughly ~$900–$1,000 saved per agent per year — about $46K/yr at 50 agents and $92K/yr at 100 agents. Beyond dollars: no third-party SaaS added to the FedRAMP boundary (avoids extra vendor ATO/ConMon overhead), data stays in the gov enclave, and there is no annual per-seat price escalation.

Assumptions: 25 agents; Atlassian Government Cloud list-price estimates (commercial cloud is lower, gov/FedRAMP tiers run higher and require annual commitment); Anchor hosting ≈ $30/month (Azure Gov App Service + container + Postgres). Mail rides existing M365 licensing. Actuals depend on tier, term, and negotiated pricing.

Open Gap — Project Management App Needed ACTION

To fully retire Jira, we still need a project / work-management application with kanban boards. Anchor today replaces Jira Service Management (ticketing) and Confluence (knowledge base), but it does not yet cover the team/project tracking that Jira Software provides — backlogs, sprints, and kanban boards for engineering and project work.

Until that piece exists, the ~$16/agent/month Jira Software line cannot be eliminated, so a portion of Atlassian spend (or an interim tool) remains. Recommended next investment: build kanban / work-management into Anchor (or stand up a companion app) so the full savings above are realized and the platform is a single system of record.
